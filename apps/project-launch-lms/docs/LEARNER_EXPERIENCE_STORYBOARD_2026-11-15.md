# Project Launch Lab — Two-Module Learner Experience Storyboard (Working v0.1)

**November 15, 2026 — instructional implementation draft for Peter's review.** Derived from the Dr. Evans-approved design blueprint. This is original course copy and interaction specification for development, **not** a claim that the LMS currently displays or saves all steps. Keep the approved blueprint itself unchanged.

## Design promise

**Welcome:** “An idea becomes a project when you make a thoughtful choice, take a manageable first step, and learn from what happens. You do not need a perfect idea, expensive equipment, a public profile, or an AI tool. In this lab, you will choose something meaningful to you, test whether it can become a project, make an achievable plan, try one safe step or document an honest obstacle, and decide what to do next.”

**Learner success is not a successful result.** It is a defensible project choice, a feasible and safe first move, honest evidence, thoughtful feedback use, and an informed next decision.

**Safety and agency panel (reusable on every task):**
- Choose a project topic you can discuss without revealing private or sensitive information.
- No learner must buy materials, post publicly, share a personal contact detail, interact with strangers, travel, or use generative AI.
- If a first step needs a trusted adult, permission, tools you lack, or a safe space, choose an alternative or document the barrier. You will not lose credit because the planned action is impractical or unsafe to perform.
- AI may help you brainstorm or critique if available and permitted, but the judgment and evidence are yours. You can complete every step without AI.
- You may use written, visual, audio-with-transcript, or accessible text-equivalent evidence as allowed by the facilitator; avoid submitting photos of people or identifiable places.

### Orientation (before Module 1)

**Screen O1 — Start here (2–3 minutes).** Acknowledge two-module journey. Show the outcomes in learner language:
- M1: *What matters to me? Which direction is realistic? How will I explain my choice?*
- M2: *What is one safe first step? What does evidence and feedback tell me? What should I do next?*
- “You can revise a choice. Your project belongs to you.”

**Screen O2 — Entry diagnostic (ungraded, 5–8 minutes).** Ask learner to respond, individually:
1. “List two interests, questions, or problems you are curious about. These can be small.”
2. “Remember something you wanted to do or learn that took a few steps. What helped you begin? What got in the way?”
3. “Compare these two directions: ‘Make something that helps everyone’ and ‘Create a two-minute guide showing one beginner how to repair a loose bicycle chain with a trusted adult’s help.’ Which could you test more easily this week? Name one constraint. You may instead choose an equivalently safe topic.”
4. “If you chose one of your interests, what is a tiny first move you might try?”
5. “How confident are you *right now* that you can move from an idea to a realistic first action? 1 = not yet confident, 5 = very confident.”

**Diagnostic feedback:** After submission, show: “A focused question usually identifies who or what the project is for, what can be tested safely, and what you could realistically do with available resources. You are not graded on your starting point. You will revisit your confidence at the end.” Provide private facilitator access to learner answers for coaching, not grading. **Implementation gap:** no current diagnostic activity or no-rubric feedback workflow.

## Module 1 — Discover & Define

**Module learning outcomes:**
1. Identify interests and questions that could become projects (M1.1).
2. Evaluate possible project directions using personal relevance, feasibility and scope (M1.2).
3. Formulate and justify one focused project direction (M1.3).

**Screen 1.1 — Hook + media example (4 minutes).** Show two contrasting first-person examples with text alternatives:
- A learner interested in drawing says, “I want to make the world more creative.” This is meaningful but too broad to test.
- The same learner narrows it: “Could a one-page illustrated guide help my younger sibling remember three steps for setting up a sketchbook?” The first possible test is to make a draft and ask the sibling, with consent, to try it.
Ask: “Which version provides a clearer first step? Why?” The answer and feedback should be visible, not a hidden grade. Offer a readable transcript/storyboard if a video is used. **Current media delivery is unverified.**

**Screen 1.2 — Three decision lenses (5 minutes).**
- *Relevance*: Why do I care enough to return to this?
- *Feasibility*: What could I actually try with my time, materials, support, access and permissions?
- *Scope*: What question is narrow enough that I can learn something from one small test?
Corrective example: A learner may value an idea highly but lack permission to run its first test. That does **not** make the interest worthless; it suggests a different first move.

**Screen 1.3 — Ungraded practice (10–15 minutes).** Use Project Launch Gauntlet if accessible; equivalent text scenario when not:
- Direction A: “Teach everyone how to eat healthy.”
- Direction B: “Draft three affordable snack ideas using food already at home and ask one consenting household member which seems most realistic.”
Prompts:
1. Enter **two ideas** you care about.
2. Compare their relevance, feasibility and scope. Identify **one tradeoff** for each.
3. Select a tentative direction and state what would make you reconsider.
4. Ask your facilitator a specific question or invite a peer to respond through the course forum.
**Learner output:** saved comparison card; **facilitator feedback:** one affirmation of evidence-based reasoning and one question that could sharpen choice. Peer feedback optional; private facilitator route is required. Prompt should **not** depend on successfully opening the Gauntlet.
**Implementation gap:** M1 ungraded activity currently cannot save/submit through the rubric-gated form.

**Screen 1.4 — Direction Brief (graded, 15–25 minutes).** Submission instructions:
- State two candidate project directions.
- Choose one and explain its personal relevance without revealing private details.
- Assess realistic time, materials, support and constraints; describe a low-resource alternative.
- Explain the scope and a testable first question or deliverable.
- Identify the tradeoff you accepted, feedback you considered, and one unresolved question.
- Include one sentence on what could make you change your direction.
**Rubric 30 points**:
1. Personal relevance (10): meaningful, specific motivation and ownership.
2. Feasibility (10): realistic conditions, resources, constraints and safe alternatives.
3. Scope and reasoning (10): focused direction, comparison and defensible tradeoff.
**Feedback contract:** facilitator publishes criterion ratings and a next-step question; learner reads before or during Module 2. Do not require a perfect or adult-approved passion.

**Screen 1.5 — Transition (2 minutes).** “You have a direction, not a lifetime commitment. In Module 2, you will test how to take a very small first step. Your choice can change when evidence changes.” Ask: “What is the biggest unknown in your direction? Save it for your first-move plan.”

## Module 2 — Plan & Launch

**Module learning outcomes:**
1. Develop a feasible plan for a small, safe first action using resources, milestones and constraints (M2.1).
2. Evaluate a draft plan against its purpose, feasibility and likely evidence, then justify an improvement *or* a reasoned decision to keep it (M2.2).
3. Attempt a bounded first action or document a genuine obstacle, interpret evidence and feedback, and justify a next step (M2.3).

**Screen 2.1 — Hook + Maya worked example (8–10 minutes).** “Maya wants to make a short rhythm guide for a school music group. Instead of making a whole lesson, she records a few beats and checks if one cue is understandable.” Play the existing **First Move Studio** three-decision scenario. Each choice reveals explanatory feedback; an audio sample has a written audio description. Debrief:
- What did Maya hope to learn?
- What was the smallest informative test?
- What did the evidence actually show?
- How did she distinguish a failure of test conditions from failure of the whole idea?
- Which feedback would help her choose a next move?
**Original media exists in code; keyboard/audio and alternative-format QA still required.**

**Screen 2.2 — Plan the bounded action (10–15 minutes).** Introduce a partially worked plan for Maya with time, resources, permission, what evidence would be useful, and a contingency. Then offer this editable **First Move Card**:
- My project direction:
- My purpose / whom or what it serves:
- The smallest **safe** first action I can try:
- What I want to learn (not merely accomplish):
- Time window / first checkpoint:
- Materials, permission, trusted support, and low-resource alternative:
- Possible constraint or risk and a safe substitute:
- Evidence I can collect without sharing sensitive information:
- What would count as *useful information*, even if the action does not work:
- One question for feedback:
**Learner output:** ungraded submitted card with revision permission semantics to be designed. Avoid making a learner disclose a name, address, photo, or private life circumstance.

**Screen 2.3 — Peer/facilitator critique (5–10 minutes).** Provide a short protocol:
1. “I can see your plan aims to learn __.”
2. “The first step seems / does not yet seem feasible because __.”
3. “A constraint you might plan for is __.”
4. “One question that could sharpen your evidence is __.”
The facilitator returns one concrete question and one suggestion; peer input is optional. Learner then answers: “I changed __ because __,” *or* “I kept __ because evidence __ supports it.” **Do not score compliance with the suggestion; score evidence-based reasoning.**
**Implementation gap:** ungraded submission + dedicated facilitator feedback not wired end to end.

**Screen 2.4 — Try, observe, or document a real obstacle (variable time).**
- Route A: Safely attempt the first step. Record action, observations, unexpected result, limitations, and one piece of non-sensitive evidence.
- Route B: If action could not be attempted safely or feasibly, record what blocked it, why you did not force it, what you learned about conditions, and an alternative test. This is equally legitimate evidence.
- Neither route requires public posting, outside contact, purchases, travel, an AI tool or a fabricated “success.”
Ask: “What do you now know, and what *still* needs testing?”

**Screen 2.5 — Launch Portfolio (graded, 25–40 minutes plus attempt).** Assemble:
1. Project purpose and direction (may be refined from M1).
2. Clear plan, milestones, resources, permissions/constraints, and safe first move.
3. Evidence of attempt **or** authentic obstacle and limitation, labeled clearly.
4. Feedback received, critique response (revision or justified retention), and reasoned next action.
5. A short transfer statement: “Where else might this plan → test → learn process help me?”
**Rubric 60 points (four x 15):**
- Purpose and planning coherence (15): intention, first action and evidence question align.
- Resources and feasibility (15): realistic scope, time/resources, constraints, safe alternatives.
- Initial action and evidence (15): honest bounded action or obstacle, observations/limits distinguished from assumptions.
- Feedback and revision (15): evaluates feedback and evidence, justifies improvement/retention and next step.
**Implementation mismatch:** Current DB rubric contains only 3 criteria x 10, total 30. Do not claim aligned until corrected and tested.

**Screen 2.6 — Knowledge check (10–15 minutes).** Existing ten-item scenario quiz is the authoritative implementation; show feedback that explains *why* each response is defensible or needs reconsideration. The quiz tests transfer of the decision lenses and evidence, not memorization or luck. Confirm grading settings, attempts and feedback visibility using synthetic role sessions. Do not duplicate a second quiz without a requirement.

**Screen 2.7 — Exit and transfer (5 minutes).**
- “How confident are you now that you can turn an idea into a feasible first action?” Same 1–5 anchor as entry.
- “One strategy I used that I could apply to another project is __.”
- “My next safe project move will be __. If a barrier appears, my alternative is __.”
Explain that **confidence change is self-report, not proof of competence**; grades rely on the authentic performance rubric.
- “Complete the short optional/non-graded course experience survey. Your feedback helps improve the lab.”

## Distinct instructional feedback loops

| Output | First feedback | Learner's response | Evidence in final work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry diagnostic | Explanatory model; facilitator can see where coaching is needed | Compare starting idea with later choice | Confidence reflection / direction change |
| M1 comparison | Gauntlet/scenario response + facilitator prompt | Refine or justify candidate | Direction Brief |
| M1 Direction Brief | Criterion-specific scored faculty review | Carry useful question to M2 | Project purpose / scope |
| M2 First Move Card | Maya worked-example explanation + facilitator critique; optional peer prompt | Improve plan or defend retention | Portfolio before/after rationale |
| First attempt or obstacle | Observation, limits and authentic constraints; facilitator may coach | Choose next safe test | Portfolio evidence/next move |
| Launch Portfolio | Four-criterion feedback | Interpret result and plan later revision | Future OWLL development, beyond this course |
| Ten-item quiz | Correct/incorrect explanation | Identify concept to revisit | Course knowledge-check record |
| Survey | No grade | Learner contributes to design improvement | Course revision, not learner score |

## Accessibility, learner agency, and media acceptance tests

- All on-screen instructions and original audio/media have text-equivalent or transcript, and captions for any video.
- All interactions work with keyboard, visibly focused controls and mobile viewport.
- A learner can pass through the entire course using an accessible, low-bandwidth text pathway without optional Vercel Gauntlet or AI tools.
- Every prompt asks for authentic learner judgment, output and evidence; no step demands costly resources, unsafe behavior, strangers or public posting.
- Both graded rubrics are visible before submission. Formative critique is distinguishable from score publication. Scores do not appear on ungraded work.
- The activity/assignment pages say what to do, how long it roughly takes, what is submitted, how feedback arrives, and what is next.
- Course navigation makes the two modules and “where I am” clear; no dead-end after an activity.
- Demonstrate a legitimate obstacle route in at least one acceptance test.
