# OWLL Website & Brand Architecture v2.0
**Review document.** This specifies a proposed public identity and implementation plan. It neither changes the organization's legal status nor proves its educational efficacy. Future products, public resources, services and research programs remain conditional.

## 1. Why this revision exists
The July 2026 website correctly established OWLL as an early-stage independent education research and design initiative, with First Landing and LOCUS as primary public prototypes. The October 11 Learning Environments update extended the real-world thesis into physical spaces. But the current homepage and metadata emphasize *place-based youth learning* and *emerging software* more than the full mission: learner agency across the lifespan, educational sovereignty, family and adult learning, community-connected opportunity, and practical public resources. The homepage's principal calls to action invite a conversation or browsing projects, not useful direct participation.

**Desired shift:** From "a lab designing an interesting kind of education" to "a rigorous, welcoming, public-facing learning organization helping people take greater ownership of their education and development."

**Non-goals:** Do not remake OWLL as a homeschool brand, political campaign, commercial room-installation company, unaccredited school, finished LOCUS product, or authoritative research institute with unsupported credentials. Do not publish experimental learner outcomes as established fact.

## 2. Brand foundations
**North star:** The world is the curriculum. Human agency is the purpose.

**Draft mission:** Open World Learning Lab investigates, designs, and shares ways for people to turn curiosity, everyday experience, focused practice, human connection, and meaningful projects into lifelong learning and greater agency.

**Draft aspiration:** Individuals, families and communities can meaningfully shape their learning pathways, gain access to diverse opportunities, develop durable knowledge and capabilities, and participate more fully in the world.

**Working definition of educational sovereignty:** The practical capacity, supported by meaningful opportunity and responsible relationships, to understand, shape, pursue, and revise one's own educational development. For children, agency develops progressively alongside adult duties of care, safety and access to plural viewpoints. Sovereignty does **not** imply isolation, immunity from evidence, neglect of foundational competencies, or that individual determination alone resolves structural barriers.

**Signature idea:** The world is the curriculum.
**Complement:** Learning belongs to the learner.
**Product principle:** Structured freedom, not mere self-directed content consumption.
**Social principle:** Raise the floor, widen paths, do not impose a ceiling.
**Research principle:** Demonstrate learning and limits; never confuse a prototype, publication, attractive artwork, or engagement metric with improved outcomes.

Brand must consistently convey six paired qualities:
1. Wonder **and** intellectual rigor.
2. Freedom **and** meaningful structure.
3. Individual agency **and** human community.
4. Universal access **and** room for excellence.
5. Human experience **and** thoughtful technology.
6. Ambition **and** epistemic humility.

## 3. Audiences and entry journeys
People arrive with different needs. OWLL must not require them to adopt one ideology, schooling choice, platform, or expensive product.

| Audience | Arrival question | Honest now-path | Future, only when ready |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adult/self-directed learner | Can I take greater ownership of my learning? | Plain-language approach, published ideas, forthcoming reviewed starter resource | Adaptive guided journeys, Field Notes and personal continuity |
| Parent/family | How can we learn well together without becoming a full-time teacher? | Parent-accessible explanation, low-cost activity examples once reviewed | Adult/family resource library and optional support |
| Educator/mentor | How do I turn curiosity into substantive learning? | Learning loop, First Landing *design/prototype* case, public brief | Reusable journey authoring tools, facilitator materials |
| Researcher/design collaborator | What questions and evidence support this? | Methods, scope and status labels, contact | Research notes, transparent evaluation and open protocols |
| Community organization | Can this work with our people, places and barriers? | Exploration of place, human support and access; no implied partnerships | Verified community journeys and authorized pilots |
| Potential patron/service customer | Could a learning environment be designed for us? | Learning Environments research concept, explicitly **not** an operating installation offer | Scoped service only if validated and actually launched |

**Visitor comprehension test:** Within 15 seconds, a first-time visitor should know what OWLL is, who it includes, why its approach is different, what can be used **today**, and what remains proposed.

## 4. Message hierarchy
**Tier 1: Human purpose** — people becoming increasingly capable, autonomous, connected learners.
**Tier 2: Learning approach** — encounter, question, investigate, retrieve, practice, apply, reflect, create, return.
**Tier 3: Work of the lab** — research and knowledge translation; practical experiences/resources; human-centered enabling infrastructure.
**Tier 4: Concrete examples** — First Landing / Water Writes the Landscape, LOCUS, Learning Environments, public briefs.
**Tier 5: Technical details** — Journey Studio, Learner Atlas, Field Notes, creator contracts, AR horizon.

Never make the technical vision the prerequisite for understanding OWLL. Explain what the learner does before showing the system that may support it.

**Eyebrow:** Independent learning research and design
**Headline:** Learning should begin with the world.
**Support:** Open World Learning Lab explores how people of all ages can turn curiosity, lived experience, practice, and meaningful projects into lasting knowledge and greater agency. We develop learning experiences, share ideas, and build toward tools that support learning throughout life.

### Proposed thesis intro (copy-ready)
**Eyebrow:** The purpose
**Headline:** Education is bigger than school, and learning lasts a lifetime.
**Body:** Learning happens through places, work, family, communities, books, tools, conversations, and questions. OWLL explores how to turn those experiences into knowledge that learners can explain, use, revisit, and build upon. The goal is not to remove guidance or foundational skills. It is to make meaningful learning more accessible, connected, and increasingly learner-directed.
**Callout:** The world is the curriculum. Human agency is the purpose.

### Proposed audience section (copy-ready)
**Eyebrow:** Who this work is for
**Title:** A learning life belongs to more than one institution.
- **Individuals:** Develop the practices and confidence to direct your own learning.
- **Families:** Learn with children while recognizing that parents are learners, too.
- **Educators and mentors:** Build rigorous learning around questions, projects, experiences and human relationships.
- **Communities and researchers:** Investigate what makes learning possible, accessible, meaningful and lasting.
**Status note:** These are people the lab intends to serve, not a claim that matching programs and services are already operating.

### Voice and language
- Warm, clear, intellectually serious, curious, grounded, invitational; avoid evangelism that requires assent to a doctrine.
- Prefer verbs: notice, explore, question, practice, build, explain, remember, participate.
- Prefer "explores," "designs," "tests," "proposes," "prototype" until evidence warrants stronger claims.
- Avoid unqualified "revolutionize," "proven," "transform every child," "personalized to every learner," "exclusive," "AI-powered education" as a default brand promise.
- Avoid treating parents with fewer resources as deficits. Acknowledge time, transport, stable housing, technology and support as real constraints without assuming lack of curiosity or capacity.
- Distinguish learning outcomes from polished artifacts. Speak about children with progressive agency and safety, not parental control alone.

## 7. Identity and art direction
**Preserve:** Living Field Atlas design language: field paper #F2EFE6, ink #17201D, deep field #102620, water #2F6672, moss #526B50, editorial Newsreader, Inter and IBM Plex Mono, restrained cartographic geometry, meaningful white space, diagram-first craftsmanship.

**Expand without abrupt rebrand:**
- Human work and relationships: adults learning, parent/child shared inquiry, mentors, communities, making and reflection, always honest about depicted participants.
- Artifacts of understanding: field journals, annotations, tools, prototypes, sources, explained models and finished learner work with permission.
- Situational breadth: home, kitchen, streets, library, park, work, studio, museum, field.
- Contrast of scales: world/landscape, learner/action, evidence/detail, wider systems.
- Visual provenance: caption images as conceptual illustration, licensed imagery, simulated prototype, or documentary evidence; never mix these invisibly.
- Avoid a luxury-home aesthetic swallowing the accessible public mission; avoid generic AI gradients, faux academia, decorative badges, overcrowded diagrams and empty inspiration.
- Don't change logo/type or palette before validating legibility and consistency.

**Photography policy:** Permission and protection before identifiable people; especially stringent for children. Do not use photos of employer-linked students, minors, or community participants without verified rights and consent/approval. Use clearly labeled conceptual or licensed imagery until documentation is available.

## 8. Publication, evidence and trust architecture
Content objects should distinguish: public essay, philosophy/working paper, practical activity, prototype demonstration, research memo, study/evaluation, product release and service page. Each publication should have stable title, type, author, date, version, status, sources, license, limitations, and related projects.

Reuse existing OWLL Research Charter evidence levels when appropriate:
- L0 conceptual proposition
- L1 grounded synthesis
- L2 technical/design demonstration
- L3 feasibility/usability evidence
- L4 learning/outcome evidence
- L5 comparative/causal evidence
- L6 independent corroboration
These belong in explanations and publication metadata, not as colorful decorative badges. Never translate a prototype's technical L2 maturity into an L4 learning-outcome claim.

The October 11 adult-first Educational Sovereignty Framework v0.1 and `Your World, Your Learning` starter kit exist as private/review-stage artifacts in the originating ChatGPT conversation; they are **not yet public OWLL Releases**, nor in this website repo. No link/button should pretend otherwise. Publishing requires founder editorial approval, source review, accessible export, appropriate license and clear version/status text.

## 9. Inclusive resource design standards
- Multiple starting modes: quick five-minute activity, full exploration, optional deeper/advanced inquiry.
- Equipment-neutral: paper/phone/offline; do not require paid AI, a dedicated room, car or specialized materials.
- Support self-directed adults as well as families; "parents are learners too" should be explicit.
- Offer translation/readability, screen-reader friendly semantic structure, meaningful alt text, downloadable text equivalents where practical.
- Safety: age-appropriate supervision and consent, no youth data collection without separate protections.
- Preserve intellectual pluralism and right to challenge sources; do not personalize into an epistemic bubble.
- Avoid false promises about behavior change or structural barriers solved by self-help alone.

## 10. Technical, accessibility and QA
Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA as a design target, without claiming certification. Token contrast review: original muted metadata #6B756F on #F2EFE6 measured ~4.15:1, below the 4.5:1 normal-text target. A proposed #59655E revision measures ~5.30:1 on field paper and ~4.67:1 on soft paper. This is a limited palette check, **not** a full WCAG audit. Review remaining opacity-based text, actual rendered image overlays, 200% zoom, mobile around 320/375/390/768/1280px, screen-reader headings/landmarks, link purposes, keyboard navigation, visible focus, reduced motion and image/dialog behavior. Existing responsive source code is not a substitute for browser testing.

Check: every route and internal link, metadata/social share, sitemap/canonical URL, data safety, typography, media crop, loading speed and Lighthouse/real-user performance where measurable. Protect natural proportions and caption legibility on mobile; don't rely on tiny embedded words in raster diagrams.

Treat the homepage as an information experience with a single dominant human-level promise, not a dashboard or list of every initiative.

## 11. Staged implementation proposal
**P0 — Now, in review:** Brand Architecture v2 specification; targeted editorial proof-of-concept on a non-production PR for hero, thesis, audiences, About, and equity language. No new routes/services, no production merge.
**P1 — After founder/content review:** Accurate First Landing and LOCUS status updates, navigation/content cleanup, mobile/accessibility QA.
**P2 — After first real resource passes review:** Publish one practical free resource and educational sovereignty working paper with source/version/license metadata; only then expose Learning navigation and "Explore free resources" primary CTA.
**P3 — After learning/visitor feedback:** Improve research/publication browsing, collected feedback and evidence; optional richer experience prototypes and structured evaluation only with proper authorization.
**P4 — Later, if validated:** Real product/creator/community/service integration; distinct availability and safeguarding controls.

Stop/rollback conditions: mission becomes less comprehensible to an ordinary adult; prototypes appear production-ready; extra navigation points to empty pages; public CTAs promise unlaunched services; accessibility worsens; learning outcome claims exceed evidence; resources imply youth data collection; or the new aesthetic drifts to generic SaaS/luxury branding.
